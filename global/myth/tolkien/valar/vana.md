# Vána

> Vána is ever young, and flowers spring where her feet touch the earth.

*Valar — Valier; the Ever Young*

## Overview

Vána is the sister of Yavanna and companion of Oromë, associated with beauty, youth and awakening.

## Identity

- **Title:** Queen of the Spring
- **Role:** Youth, spring, flowers and renewal
- **Spouse / consort:** Oromë
- **Familiar symbols:** flowers; springtime; birds
- **Kindred:** Valier

## Names and Etymology

The name **Vána** is used throughout Tolkien's published legends for this figure.
Where an alternative name is known, it is preserved here as a secondary epithet rather than treated as a separate character.

## Character and Domain

In the Ainulindalë and Valaquenta, Vána is defined less as a mortal-style personage than as a divine power: a will, office and creative theme made visible.
The sphere associated with Vána is **Youth, spring, flowers and renewal**. Tolkien repeatedly presents the Valar through consorts, crafts, music, duties and the shape of their houses.

## Relationship to Middle-earth

The Valar are the Powers of the World who entered Arda after the Music of the Ainur. They oppose Melkor, preserve creation and generally act through delegated powers rather than constant direct rule.

## Selected Quotes

> Vána is ever young, and flowers spring where her feet touch the earth.

*The Silmarillion, Valaquenta.*

> The Valar, the Guardians of the World, are called the Powers of the World; they are the greatest of the Ainur who came to Arda.

*The Silmarillion, Valaquenta (condensed).*

> The Music of the Ainur brought Eä into being, and into its vision the Valar entered.

*The Silmarillion, Ainulindalë (condensed).*
