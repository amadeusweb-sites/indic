# Siege of Angband

*The long containment of Morgoth's fortress · 60–455 of the First Age*

---

## Overview

After Dagor Aglareb, the Noldor and their allies contained Angband, Morgoth's stronghold beneath Thangorodrim. The siege was a strategic blockade rather than a continuous assault on the fortress. It restricted Morgoth's open movement for centuries, but did not destroy his strength; he eventually broke the encirclement in the Dagor Bragollach.

## Causes and Context

The Noldor's victory in the [Dagor Aglareb](../battles/dagor-aglareb.md) secured the northern approaches and enabled them to watch Angband. The aim was to contain Morgoth and deny his armies free passage into Beleriand, not to storm a fortress the Elves could not take.

The long period of relative security allowed the Elven kingdoms to grow. It also gave Morgoth time to increase his forces and prepare a new offensive. The siege was therefore a strategic pause in the war, not proof that Angband had been neutralized.

---

## Course of the Siege

1. **The blockade begins.** After Dagor Aglareb, the Noldor established a watch around Angband and held the northern approaches.
2. **The siege settles into a long containment.** The Elves kept Morgoth's forces largely confined, while their own realms developed across Beleriand.
3. **Morgoth builds his strength.** Angband remained supplied with arms and forces; the blockade did not prevent Morgoth from preparing for another war.
4. **The siege is broken.** In 455, rivers of fire issued from Thangorodrim and Morgoth's armies attacked in the [Dagor Bragollach](../battles/dagor-bragollach.md).
5. **The balance of power changes.** The northern lands were overrun and the long containment ended.

## Outcome and Significance

The siege delayed Morgoth's expansion and provided the Noldorin kingdoms with a long period in which to strengthen their positions. Its eventual collapse exposed the limits of containment: Angband remained powerful, and Morgoth chose when to break the blockade.

The [Dagor Bragollach](../battles/dagor-bragollach.md) ended the siege and transformed the military and political balance of Beleriand. The siege should not be read as a continuous battle lasting centuries; it was an extended strategic encirclement punctuated by the events of the wider war.
