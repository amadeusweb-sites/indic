# Siege of Barad-dûr

*The Last Alliance's siege of Sauron's tower · 3434–3441 of the Second Age*

---

## Overview

The Siege of Barad-dûr followed the Last Alliance's victory at Dagorlad. The allied Elves and Men entered Mordor and surrounded Sauron's fortress, seeking to end his rule rather than merely drive his army from the field. The siege lasted seven years and ended when Sauron came forth to fight.

## Causes and Context

The Last Alliance entered Mordor after winning the [Battle of Dagorlad](../battles/dagorlad.md). Barad-dûr was the center of Sauron's power, so the Alliance had to invest the fortress rather than rely on its earlier victory in the field.

The seven-year siege exhausted both sides and cost the lives of important leaders. Anárion was killed before the final confrontation. Sauron remained inside the tower until he came out to fight the leaders of the Alliance.

---

## Course of the Siege

1. **The Alliance enters Mordor.** After Dagorlad, the allied host passed through the Black Gate and surrounded Barad-dûr.
2. **The siege continues for seven years.** The fortress resisted the attackers, and the Alliance suffered heavy losses.
3. **Anárion is killed.** He fell during the siege before the final struggle with Sauron.
4. **Sauron comes forth.** At the end of the siege, Sauron fought Gil-galad and Elendil.
5. **The leaders fall and Sauron is overthrown.** Gil-galad and Elendil were killed. Isildur cut the One Ring from Sauron's hand after they had fallen.
6. **The tower is destroyed.** With Sauron's defeat, Barad-dûr was overthrown, though the Ring survived.

---

## Outcome and Significance

The fall of Barad-dûr ended Sauron's bodily rule in the Second Age, but not the power bound to the Ring. Isildur kept the Ring rather than destroying it. Sauron's power was diminished and hidden, yet he was able to return much later.

The siege was the final phase of the War of the Last Alliance, following the field victory at Dagorlad. The death of Isildur at the [Gladden Fields](../battles/gladden-fields.md) and the Ring's loss there belong to the aftermath, not to the siege.

## Editorial Note

Barad-dûr's fall did not destroy the One Ring. The siege ended Sauron's Second Age rule, but the Ring's survival left open the possibility of his return.
