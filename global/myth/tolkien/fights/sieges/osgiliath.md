# Siege of Osgiliath

*The struggle for Gondor's river crossing · 3018 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

Osgiliath was Gondor's ancient capital and the principal crossing of the Anduin. Sauron's assault in 3018 forced Gondor's defenders from the eastern side of the city. They destroyed the bridge to slow the enemy's advance, but Osgiliath remained a contested approach to Minas Tirith.

## Causes and Context

Osgiliath's bridges made it the most direct crossing between Mordor's forces and Gondor's western defenses. The city had been partly ruined for centuries, so the defense concerned the river line and the remaining positions rather than a functioning capital.

The 3018 attack began the open war in Gondor. The later crossing in 3019 was a separate operation that preceded the siege of Minas Tirith. This entry focuses on the defense and loss of the river crossing; the initial engagement is also described in the [Battle of Osgiliath](../battles/osgiliath.md).

---

## Course of the Siege

1. **Sauron's forces attack across the Anduin.** The enemy crossed under cover of darkness and surprise, striking Gondor's defenses in the ruined city.
2. **The defenders contest the bridge and east bank.** Boromir and Faramir were among the captains involved in holding the crossing.
3. **Gondor withdraws west.** The defenders were eventually forced from the eastern side of Osgiliath.
4. **The bridge is destroyed.** Gondor broke the crossing to prevent Sauron's forces from advancing over it in strength.
5. **The city remains exposed.** Sauron retained the east bank and later crossed the river again as part of the 3019 assault on Minas Tirith.

| Side | Leaders and peoples | Part in the siege |
| Gondor | Boromir, Faramir, and the defenders of Osgiliath | Contested the crossing and withdrew after destroying the bridge |
| Mordor | Sauron's forces | Crossed the Anduin and took the eastern side of the city |

---

## Outcome and Significance

The loss of the eastern side of Osgiliath opened the route toward Minas Tirith, but destroying the bridge prevented the enemy from using the intact crossing. Gondor held the western bank and continued to defend its capital until the much larger assault of 3019.

Osgiliath's defense should not be confused with the siege of Minas Tirith. The city was an important river position, not an intact capital, and the 3018 contest did not itself bring about Minas Tirith's fall.
