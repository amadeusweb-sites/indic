# Siege of Minas Tirith

*The siege and relief of Gondor's chief city · 13–15 March 3019*

---

## Overview

The Siege of Minas Tirith was Sauron's main assault on Gondor during the War of the Ring. Mordor's forces crossed the Anduin, encircled the city, and brought Grond against the Great Gate. The defenders held until the Rohirrim arrived and the siege turned into the Battle of the Pelennor Fields.

## Causes and Context

The siege was the culmination of Sauron's campaign to break Gondor before the West could unite against him. Gondor's defenders had already suffered losses at Osgiliath and along the Anduin. The city's walls and successive levels provided a strong defense, but Mordor's army greatly outnumbered the garrison.

Denethor directed the defense until despair overcame him. Gandalf took command as the enemy reached the city. Rohan was bound by its alliance with Gondor and rode to relieve Minas Tirith, while Aragorn moved north by river with forces from the southern fiefs.

---

## Course of the Siege

1. **Mordor crosses the Anduin.** Sauron's army passed the river and advanced across the Pelennor toward Minas Tirith.
2. **The city is encircled.** The attackers surrounded the walls and brought engines, including the great ram Grond, against the Great Gate.
3. **The outer defenses are breached.** Grond broke the Gate, but Gandalf and the defenders continued to resist within the city.
4. **The Rohirrim arrive.** At dawn on 15 March, Théoden's riders reached the Pelennor and attacked the besieging army.
5. **The siege becomes a field battle.** The Rohirrim broke the siege lines. The arrival of Aragorn's fleet and southern forces later completed the relief in the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../battles/pelennor-fields.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The siege ended with Minas Tirith still in Gondor's hands, but only after a costly battle. Denethor died during the crisis, Faramir survived, and the arrival of Rohan prevented the city from falling. Aragorn's ships brought further reinforcements from the south.

The relief did not end the War of the Ring. Sauron's forces remained dangerous, and the captains of the West later marched to the Black Gate to divert Sauron's attention from Frodo and Sam. The siege and the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../battles/pelennor-fields.md) are consecutive phases, not the same event.
