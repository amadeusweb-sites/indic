# Dagor Aglareb

*The Glorious Battle · 60 of the First Age*

---

## Overview

Dagor Aglareb was a major victory for the Noldor and their allies over Morgoth's Orcs. The defeat of the invading host secured the northern approaches to Beleriand and began the long Siege of Angband. The victory gave the Elves time to fortify their realms, but it did not remove Morgoth or end the war.

## Causes and Context

After returning to Middle-earth, the Noldor established kingdoms across Beleriand and kept watch on Morgoth's stronghold in Angband. Morgoth tested their defenses with a large assault. The attack was an attempt to break the new Elvish power before its realms and alliances could become secure.

The Noldor's response was coordinated enough to meet the Orcs across the northern front. Their victory is called “Glorious,” but the name should not be read as a final triumph over Morgoth. Angband remained intact, and Morgoth continued to command forces from within it.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Morgoth sends out his host.** Orcs advanced from Angband against the northern defenses of the Noldor and their allies.
2. **The Elves meet the attack.** The defenders struck the invading force from more than one direction, preventing it from gaining a foothold in Beleriand.
3. **The Orcs are routed.** The host was broken and driven back toward Angband. Tolkien's account emphasizes the scale of the defeat but does not preserve a detailed sequence of unit movements or named commanders.
4. **The siege begins.** The victory enabled the Noldor to contain Angband and establish the long watch known as the Siege of Angband.

## Outcome and Significance

Dagor Aglareb changed the conflict from an open assault into a prolonged containment of Angband. The Noldor gained security to strengthen their positions and watch the northern approaches. The period that followed is called the Siege of Angband, though it was not a complete encirclement that prevented Morgoth from gathering strength.

Morgoth used the years of the siege to prepare a new offensive. In 455 of the First Age, he broke the blockade in the [Dagor Bragollach](./dagor-bragollach.md). Dagor Aglareb was therefore a real strategic success, but not the defeat of Angband or a guarantee of lasting peace.
