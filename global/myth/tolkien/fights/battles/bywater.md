# Battle of Bywater

*The Hobbits' defense of the Shire · 3 November 3019*

---

## Overview

The Battle of Bywater was the final armed conflict of the War of the Ring. When Frodo, Sam, Merry, and Pippin returned to the Shire, they found it controlled by ruffians acting under Saruman's authority. The Hobbits organized a rising, and Merry and Pippin led the defense that defeated the ruffians near Bywater.

## Causes and Context

While the Ring-bearers were away, Saruman's agents had taken control of the Shire. Rules, searches, confiscations, and industrial changes damaged Hobbit homes and livelihoods. The returning Travellers found that the Shire's familiar local government had been replaced by armed men and collaborators.

The resistance began as an effort to warn and organize the Hobbits, not as a planned battle. By the time the Travellers reached Bywater, the ruffians had been driven toward a confrontation. The fight was local in scale, but it completed the return journey's story: the Shire itself had to be freed after the defeat of Sauron.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The Travellers find the Shire under occupation.** The returning Hobbits saw the damage caused by Saruman's rule and began rallying resistance in the villages.
2. **The Shire-folk organize.** Merry and Pippin helped turn scattered opposition into a coordinated force. Hobbits from across the Shire gathered to confront the ruffians.
3. **The ruffians are contained near Bywater.** The Hobbits used barriers and their knowledge of local roads to prevent the larger force from moving freely through the village.
4. **The Hobbits win.** The ruffians were defeated, and the organized occupation of the Shire ended. Frodo urged restraint and tried to prevent needless revenge.
5. **Saruman's rule ends.** Saruman was not killed in the battle. He was later released by Frodo and then murdered at Bag End by Gríma Wormtongue.

## Outcome and Significance

The victory ended Saruman's control of the Shire, but it did not undo the harm caused by his occupation. The Hobbits had to repair homes, mills, and fields after the fighting. The episode shows that the effects of the War of the Ring reached beyond the great battlefields and into ordinary communities.

Frodo's refusal to seek vengeance distinguishes the outcome from a simple revenge story. He tried to spare defeated enemies, even after the violence inflicted on the Shire. The Battle of Bywater concludes the [Scouring of the Shire](../wars/ring.md), while Saruman's death occurs afterward and is not part of this battle.

## Editorial Note
