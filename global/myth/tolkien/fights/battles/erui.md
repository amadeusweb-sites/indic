# Battle of the Crossings of Erui

*Eldacar's return during the Kin-strife · 1447 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of the Crossings of Erui was the decisive field engagement in Eldacar's return to Gondor during the Kin-strife. Castamir had displaced Eldacar and taken the throne. Eldacar returned from exile with allies from Rhovanion, defeated Castamir's army near the River Erui, and regained the crown. Castamir was killed, but his sons and supporters continued the resistance at Pelargir.

## Causes and Context

The Kin-strife began when Castamir rebelled against Eldacar, whose mother was a Northwoman. Castamir used Eldacar's mixed ancestry to challenge his claim, drove him from Gondor, and took the throne. Eldacar spent years in exile among his mother's people in Rhovanion, while Castamir's rule deepened the divisions within the kingdom.

Eldacar's return brought Northman allies into Gondor to restore him. The decisive battle took place at the Crossings of Erui, but defeating Castamir in the field did not immediately end the rebellion: his sons and surviving followers retreated to Pelargir.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Eldacar returns from exile.** He gathered support among the Northmen of Rhovanion and led an army south to reclaim the throne.
2. **The armies meet at Erui.** Castamir's forces confronted Eldacar near the river crossings.
3. **Eldacar defeats the usurper.** The returning king's army overcame Castamir's supporters. Castamir was killed in the battle.
4. **The rebels retreat to Pelargir.** Castamir's sons and many followers escaped south to the port, where they continued to resist Eldacar.
5. **The Kin-strife enters its final phase.** Eldacar was restored, but the survivors later fled by sea to Umbar after the separate [Battle of Pelargir](./pelargir.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The victory restored Eldacar to the throne and ended Castamir's personal rule. It did not erase the divisions of the Kin-strife or immediately defeat all of Castamir's supporters. His sons maintained a last holdout at Pelargir before fleeing to Umbar, where their descendants became a long-term enemy of Gondor.

Castamir died at Erui, not at Pelargir. The distinction separates the decisive field battle from the later port resistance and avoids attributing the usurper's death to the wrong engagement.
