# Dagor Bragollach

*The Battle of Sudden Flame · 455 of the First Age*

---

## Overview

The Dagor Bragollach broke the long Siege of Angband and ended the Noldor's containment of Morgoth in the north. At midwinter, rivers of fire poured from Thangorodrim across Ard-galen, turning the green plain into the ash-field later called Anfauglith. Morgoth's armies followed the devastation, overwhelming much of the northern defense and killing several of its leaders.

The defeat was severe, but it did not end resistance in Beleriand. Fingolfin and Fingon held Hithlum, Maedhros retained Himring, and Barahir's company survived in Dorthonion. Finrod was saved at the Fen of Serech, while Fingolfin's ride to Angband became the battle's most remembered single combat.

## Causes and Context

The Noldor had contained Morgoth behind the defenses of Angband for centuries after the [Dagor Aglareb](./dagor-aglareb.md). During the [Siege of Angband](../sieges/angband.md), Morgoth gathered strength within the fortress. The lands before Angband, especially Ard-galen, had become the chief field of the siege; the Dagor Bragollach began when Morgoth abandoned that defensive posture and launched a sudden assault.

The name of the battle reflects its opening: fire from Thangorodrim swept south across the plain, destroying the land and scattering the defenders. The attack was not a single clash at Angband's gates. It was a campaign across several fronts, from Dorthonion and the passes in the east to the approaches to Hithlum in the west.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Morgoth breaks the siege.** In the winter of 455, fire flowed from Thangorodrim over Ard-galen. The plain was burned and became Anfauglith, the Gasping Dust. The defenders' positions were disrupted before Morgoth's hosts advanced south.

2. **Dorthonion is overrun.** Orcs and other forces of Angband attacked the highlands of Dorthonion. Angrod and Aegnor, sons of Finarfin, were slain, as was Bregolas of the House of Bëor. Many of the people of the region were killed or scattered. Bregolas's brother Barahir gathered a small company and continued resistance in the wild.

3. **The eastern defenses are driven back.** Morgoth's assault also broke through the eastern approaches. The sons of Fëanor were forced from the lands they had guarded; Celegorm and Curufin fled south, while Maedhros held the fortress of Himring against the attack. His defense preserved an eastern stronghold, but the coordinated line of the siege had been broken.

4. **The western front is attacked.** Morgoth's forces pressed against Hithlum and the Pass of Sirion. Hador of the House of Hador was killed defending the pass of Sirion, and his son Galdor continued the defense. Fingolfin and Fingon held their people in Hithlum, even as the scale of the defeat elsewhere became clear.

5. **Barahir saves Finrod.** At the Fen of Serech, Finrod's force was in danger of being cut off. Barahir and his men intervened and enabled Finrod to escape with a remnant of his people. In gratitude, Finrod swore an oath to aid Barahir and his kin, gave him his ring, and promised that their houses would be bound in friendship. Barahir's later resistance and Beren's appeal to Finrod grow from this episode.

6. **Fingolfin rides to Angband.** When he learned the scale of the defeat, Fingolfin rode alone to Angband and challenged Morgoth. He wounded Morgoth seven times before being struck down. Thorondor carried Fingolfin's body away from the gates; in doing so, the great eagle marred Morgoth's face. Fingolfin's son Fingon succeeded him as High King of the Noldor.

## Outcome and Significance

The Dagor Bragollach ended the Siege of Angband as a functioning containment of Morgoth. Ard-galen was destroyed, Dorthonion was lost to the Noldor, and the eastern defenders were pushed back from much of their territory. Morgoth regained the initiative and could threaten the remaining free realms from the north.

The victory was not total. Fingon maintained the defense of Hithlum, Maedhros held Himring, and Doriath and Nargothrond remained beyond Morgoth's immediate control. Barahir's small band continued the resistance in Dorthonion. Finrod's oath to Barahir later became important to Beren's quest, linking the aftermath of the battle to the story of Beren and Lúthien.

Fingolfin's death marked the loss of the Noldor's High King in Middle-earth. His single combat did not reverse the defeat, but it wounded Morgoth and demonstrated that the Dark Lord could be challenged. The battle set the conditions for the later [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](./nirnaeth-arnoediad.md), where the surviving western and eastern powers would attempt another united assault.

## Editorial Note

This page follows the published *Silmarillion* as its main account and uses *The War of the Jewels* as a comparison, not as a blended replacement text. The battle was a strategic defeat for the Noldor and their allies, but the northern realms did not all fall at once: Hithlum, Himring, Doriath, and Nargothrond survived in different conditions. Barahir's intervention at Serech is included as part of the battle's aftermath and the origin of Finrod's oath.
