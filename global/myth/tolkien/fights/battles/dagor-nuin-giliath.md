# Dagor-nuin-Giliath

*The Battle-under-Stars · 1497 of the Years of the Trees*

---

## Overview

Dagor-nuin-Giliath was the first great battle fought by Fëanor's host after the Noldor returned to Middle-earth. Morgoth sent an Orc-host against them soon after their landing at Losgar. The Noldor defeated the Orcs, but Fëanor pursued the retreating enemy too far and was mortally wounded by Gothmog.

## Causes and Context

Fëanor's followers had crossed the sea by ship and landed at Losgar. They were establishing themselves in Beleriand when Morgoth attacked. Fingolfin's host, which had crossed the Helcaraxë, had not yet joined Fëanor's people; the two groups' later arrival and settlement should not be confused with the events of this battle.

Morgoth's assault was intended to destroy the newcomers before they could organize their defense. Instead, the Noldor rallied and drove the Orcs back. The battle's name means “Battle-under-Stars,” referring to the time before the Sun and Moon.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Morgoth attacks the newcomers.** An Orc-host came against Fëanor's people in Mithrim, while they were still securing their foothold.
2. **The Noldor counterattack.** Fëanor's host rallied and drove the Orcs northward toward Angband.
3. **Fëanor presses the pursuit.** Fëanor rode ahead of his forces, continuing the pursuit beyond the protection of his host.
4. **The Balrogs turn on Fëanor.** Gothmog and other Balrogs surrounded and wounded him. His sons rescued him, but he died soon afterward from his wounds.
5. **The host regroups.** Maedhros became the leading figure among Fëanor's sons. The Noldor had won the field, but their return to Middle-earth was marked by the loss of their most forceful leader.

## Outcome and Significance

The Noldor won the first major battle of their return, securing their foothold in Beleriand. The victory did not end Morgoth's threat, and the death of Fëanor immediately changed the leadership of his people. Maedhros and his brothers inherited both their father's cause and the Oath of Fëanor, whose consequences would divide the Noldor in later conflicts.

The battle also established a contrast between military success and its cost. The Orc-host was driven off, but Fëanor's decision to continue the pursuit alone exposed him to the Balrogs. This battle is distinct from the earlier assaults on the Sindar in the [First Battle of Beleriand](./beleriand.md).
