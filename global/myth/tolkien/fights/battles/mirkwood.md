# Battle under the Trees

*The defense of northern Mirkwood · 15 March 3019*

---

## Overview

The Battle under the Trees was fought in northern Mirkwood when Sauron's forces attacked the Woodland Realm of Thranduil. The assault formed part of the War of the Ring's eastern fronts. Thranduil's people held their ground; after Sauron's defeat, the enemy was routed and the forest's future was settled by Thranduil and Celeborn.

## Causes and Context

Sauron attacked the Woodland Realm from Dol Guldur while his forces also assaulted Lórien and fought in Gondor. The eastern campaign prevented the Elves of northern Mirkwood from helping the West. The chronology names the battle but gives little detail about its conduct, the forces involved, or individual deeds.

This short account is therefore based on the recorded date and outcome, with the political settlement of the forest treated as aftermath rather than as part of the battle itself.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Sauron's forces attack the Woodland Realm.** The enemy advanced into northern Mirkwood from Dol Guldur.
2. **Thranduil's people resist.** The Wood-elves defended their land; the surviving chronology does not describe the tactical sequence or losses.
3. **Sauron's defeat changes the front.** The destruction of the One Ring broke the power sustaining the wider assault.
4. **The enemy is driven back.** The Woodland Realm survived the war, and the attackers were defeated.
5. **The forest's future is agreed.** Thranduil and Celeborn met after the war and divided the forest's lands, which were renamed Eryn Lasgalen.

## Outcome and Significance

The Woodland Realm survived the eastern assault. After Sauron's fall, Thranduil and Celeborn met to settle the future of Mirkwood, which was divided among the Wood-elves, the Beornings, and the Elves of Lórien, and renamed Eryn Lasgalen, the Wood of Greenleaves.

The attack and the three [battles of Lórien](./lorien.md) show that the War of the Ring reached both sides of the Anduin. They were part of the same strategic campaign but distinct fronts with separate defenders.
