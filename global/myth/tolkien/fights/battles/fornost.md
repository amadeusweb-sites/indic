# Battle of Fornost

*The end of Angmar's power in the north · 1975 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of Fornost was the final major defeat of Angmar. After the Witch-king had captured Fornost and destroyed the North-kingdom of Arthedain, an army from Gondor joined the surviving Dúnedain and Elves of Lindon. Their victory ended Angmar as a realm, though it did not restore Arnor.

## Causes and Context

For centuries the Witch-king of Angmar had fought the successor kingdoms of Arnor. By 1974, Arthedain was the last of those kingdoms still standing. The Witch-king captured Fornost, its capital, and Arvedui, the last king, was lost while fleeing north. The fall of the city seemed to end organized Dúnedain rule in the North.

Gondor sent a force under Eärnur across Eriador to answer the danger. The surviving Dúnedain and the Elves of Lindon joined the southern army. Their counterattack was an allied effort to destroy Angmar's power, not a campaign to restore Arthedain's monarchy.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Fornost falls.** The Witch-king took the North-kingdom's capital after Arthedain's defenses had been worn down.
2. **Gondor sends aid.** Eärnur led a Gondorian army north. The Dúnedain who remained in Eriador and Círdan's Elves of Lindon joined him.
3. **The allies meet Angmar.** The combined force confronted the Witch-king's army in the region of Fornost and routed it.
4. **The Witch-king escapes.** He fled east rather than being captured. Eärnur wished to pursue, but Glorfindel warned that the Witch-king would not fall by the hand of a man.
5. **Angmar is broken.** The Witch-king's realm was destroyed. The prophecy about his death was fulfilled much later when Éowyn and Merry faced him at the [Pelennor Fields](./pelennor-fields.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The battle ended Angmar's power, but the victory came too late to save Arthedain. The northern kingdom was not re-established, and its surviving Dúnedain became a scattered people. Gondor's intervention prevented the Witch-king from maintaining a realm in Eriador, but it could not undo the losses of the preceding wars.

The Witch-king's escape had consequences far beyond the North. Glorfindel's warning that he would not be slain by a man became a prophecy fulfilled at the Pelennor Fields, where Éowyn—assisted by Merry—defeated him. Fornost therefore links the wars of Arnor to the final conflict of the Third Age.
