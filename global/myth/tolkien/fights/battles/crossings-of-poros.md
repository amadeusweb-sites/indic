# Battle of the Crossings of Poros

*Gondor and Rohan against the Haradrim · 2885 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of the Crossings of Poros was fought when Haradrim forces crossed into Gondor's southern lands. King Folcwine of Rohan sent his sons Folcred and Fastred to honor Rohan's alliance with Gondor. The allies defeated the invaders, but both princes fell.

## Causes and Context

The Poros marked a frontier exposed to attacks from Harad. When the Haradrim invaded Gondor's southern lands, Gondor called on Rohan for aid under the long-standing alliance between the kingdoms. Folcwine answered by sending his twin sons, Folcred and Fastred, with a force of Rohirrim.

The choice to send both heirs made the alliance's cost especially clear. The battle repelled the immediate incursion, but the continuing threat from the south remained part of Gondor's later history.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The Haradrim cross the Poros.** Their force entered Gondor's southern territories and threatened the frontier.
2. **Rohan answers Gondor's call.** Folcwine sent Folcred and Fastred with Rohirrim to join the defense.
3. **The allies meet the invaders.** Gondorian and Rohirric forces fought together at the river crossings.
4. **The Haradrim are defeated.** The allied army drove the invaders back, but Folcred and Fastred were killed in the fighting.
5. **Gondor compensates Folcwine.** Gondor sent payment to Rohan in recompense for the princes' deaths. Folcwine used the wealth to strengthen his people.

---

## Outcome and Significance

The victory checked the Haradrim incursion and demonstrated that the alliance between Gondor and Rohan remained active. Folcred and Fastred's deaths deprived Folcwine of his heirs, and Gondor's recompense acknowledged the loss suffered by the royal house of Rohan.

The battle did not end Gondor's conflicts with Harad. It was a successful defense of the frontier within a longer history of southern wars, not a final settlement between the two peoples.
