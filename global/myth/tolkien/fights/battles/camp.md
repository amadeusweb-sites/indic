# Battle of the Camp

*Gondor's counterstroke against the Wainriders · 1944 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of the Camp was Gondor's decisive counterattack against the Wainriders in 1944. While the main Wainrider force camped near Mordor, Eärnil brought Gondor's southern army north and struck it. The victory ended the immediate invasion, though it followed the death of King Ondoher and his sons in a separate northern battle.

## Causes and Context

The Wainriders attacked Gondor in coordination with a southern invasion by the Haradrim. The northern army, led by King Ondoher, was overwhelmed, and both the king and his sons were killed. The disaster threatened Gondor with defeat on two fronts and left the succession uncertain.

Eärnil commanded the southern army. He first defeated the Haradrim, then turned north against the Wainriders. The Battle of the Camp was not the same engagement in which Ondoher fell; it was Eärnil's counterstroke against the main Wainrider force.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The northern army is destroyed.** Ondoher and his sons fell in the fighting against the Wainriders. The catastrophe left Gondor without its king and the main force in the north.
2. **Eärnil defeats the Haradrim.** The southern army repelled the concurrent attack from Harad, freeing Eärnil to move against the Wainriders.
3. **Eärnil marches north.** Rather than return to Minas Tirith, he led his army against the Wainriders' camp near Mordor.
4. **The camp is surprised.** Eärnil's attack caught the Wainriders off guard and destroyed their force. Gondor's immediate danger from the invasion was ended.
5. **The succession is settled.** After the victory, Eärnil was chosen as king. Arvedui of Arthedain also claimed the throne through his marriage to Ondoher's daughter, but Gondor's Council selected Eärnil.

## Outcome and Significance

Eärnil's victory ended the immediate Wainrider threat and preserved Gondor after the destruction of its northern army. The campaign's success made him a leading candidate for the vacant throne. His accession followed a considered choice by Gondor's Council, not simply his battlefield victory; Arvedui's claim was rejected.

The battle also shows why the invasion must be treated as a coordinated campaign rather than one engagement. Ondoher's death, the Haradrim defeat, and the attack on the Wainrider camp were distinct events in the same year. The earlier [Battle of the Plains](./plains.md) marks an earlier phase of Gondor's conflict with the Wainriders.

## Editorial Note

The northern battle that killed Ondoher and his sons is separate from the Battle of the Camp. Eärnil's southern victory and later strike against the Wainrider camp are treated as the counteroffensive that followed.
