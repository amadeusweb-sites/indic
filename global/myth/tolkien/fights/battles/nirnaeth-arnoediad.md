# Nirnaeth Arnoediad

*The Battle of Unnumbered Tears · 472 of the First Age*

---

## Overview

The Nirnaeth Arnoediad was the Fifth Battle of Beleriand, fought in 472 of the First Age on the plain of Anfauglith. The Union of Maedhros brought together Elves, Men, and Dwarves in a coordinated attempt to break Morgoth's power in Angband. Deception, delay, and Morgoth's release of his reserves turned the campaign into a catastrophic defeat for the allied host.

Tolkien gives the name its meaning within the story: the grief was so great that “no song or tale can contain all its grief.” The battle shattered the Union, killed Fingon and Huor, and ended with Húrin taken alive. It did not, however, destroy every Elvish refuge: Turgon escaped with survivors, and Gondolin remained hidden.

## Causes and the Union of Maedhros

After the Dagor Bragollach and the death of Fingolfin, Morgoth's forces held the initiative in Beleriand. Maedhros sought to unite the remaining peoples who opposed him and strike Angband through a coordinated attack. The plan depended on the eastern and western hosts moving together: Maedhros would advance from the east, while Fingon led the western host from Hithlum.

The alliance was broad but not uniform. It included the Noldor under Fingon and the sons of Fëanor, Turgon's people from Gondolin, the Edain of the Houses of Hador and Haleth, and the Dwarves of Belegost. The people of Bór also fought faithfully. By contrast, Ulfang and his sons had secretly entered Morgoth's service. Their treachery would strike at the Union from within.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The attack begins before the armies unite.** On the fourth day of the war, Morgoth's forces lured Fingon's host forward by displaying the captured Noldorin warrior Gelmir and killing him before his kin. Gwindor, Gelmir's brother, charged in fury; Fingon's army followed, and the western host was drawn into battle before Maedhros had arrived. Gwindor's force reached the gates of Angband, but few returned from that assault.

2. **The western host is driven back.** With Gwindor at the head, the western host pressed almost to Angband, but could not sustain the assault. Fingon's forces were forced to withdraw toward the Ered Wethrin. Maedhros was still delayed in the east by false warnings supplied through Uldor, one of Ulfang's sons.

3. **Turgon reaches Fingon.** As night fell on the fifth day, the Orcs surrounded the retreating western host, still far from the Ered Wethrin. At dawn on the sixth day, Turgon brought the army of Gondolin from the south and broke through to Fingon. His unexpected arrival restored hope and helped hold open the route toward the Pass of Sirion. Maedhros's horns were heard at the third hour of the morning; his eastern host struck Morgoth's forces from the rear.

4. **Morgoth commits his reserves.** As the Orcs began to falter, Morgoth sent out his remaining strength: wolves and wolf-riders, Balrogs, and Glaurung, the Father of Dragons. Glaurung drove between the allied hosts, splitting them apart. The betrayal of Ulfang's followers further weakened Maedhros's force, while the faithful people of Bór resisted them.

5. **The hosts are broken.** Fingon was killed by Gothmog and another Balrog. The Dwarves of Belegost fought Glaurung; their lord Azaghâl wounded the dragon before he fell, and the Dwarves withdrew with his body. The survivors of Maedhros's host escaped eastward, while Fingon's remaining people were forced toward the Pass of Sirion.

6. **Húrin and Huor hold the retreat.** Húrin and Huor stayed with Turgon long enough for the Gondolindrim to withdraw. Huor's parting words foretold that a new hope would arise from the union of their houses: Eärendil, son of Tuor and Idril. Huor was killed, and Húrin was captured alive by Morgoth's command. In the published account, Húrin's cry is remembered as “Aurë entuluva!”—“Day shall come again!”

## Outcome and Significance

The battle broke the Union of Maedhros as a functioning alliance and left Morgoth dominant across much of Beleriand. Fingon, High King of the Noldor, was dead; Huor and many of the Men of Hador fell; Húrin was carried captive to Angband; and the forces of Maedhros retreated in disarray. Haldir, lord of the Haladin, and most of the Men of Brethil who fought beside him also fell.

The defeat exposed the western lands to Morgoth's power. Hithlum was later given to the Easterlings who had served him, and the surviving people of Hador there were subjected to their rule. This should not be read as a betrayal by all Easterlings: Bór's people remained faithful, while the treachery was committed by Ulfang's faction.

The victory did not end resistance. Turgon escaped with the survivors from Gondolin, and the city remained undiscovered. Doriath also endured beyond the battle. The Nirnaeth instead marks the collapse of the great united offensive and the beginning of a darker phase in the wars of Beleriand. Húrin's capture connects the battle directly to the later story of his family, but that story is not itself a continuation of the battle.

## Editorial Note

This page follows the published *Silmarillion* account as its main narrative. The alliance was not ethnically or politically uniform: the treachery of Ulfang's faction must not be generalized to all Easterlings. The later annalistic and narrative texts are identified separately rather than silently blended into one version.
