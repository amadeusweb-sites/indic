# Battle of Erebor

*The Dwarves' defense of the Lonely Mountain · March 3019*

---

## Overview

The Battle of Erebor was the Dwarves' defense of the Lonely Mountain during Sauron's northern campaign. Dáin Ironfoot and his people fought beside Brand's Men of Dale against an Easterling army. After Brand and Dáin fell, the survivors withdrew behind Erebor's gates and endured a siege until Sauron's defeat changed the war.

## Causes and Context

The Easterling attack on Erebor was part of Sauron's coordinated war against the Free Peoples. The northern assault kept the Dwarves and Men of Dale from joining the defense of Gondor. The fighting began outside the Mountain, in Dale, where Brand and Dáin led the defense together.

When the Easterlings forced the defenders back, the survivors took refuge in Erebor. This page focuses on the Dwarves' defense and the siege; the linked [Battle of Dale](./dale.md) covers the fighting in the city and Brand's role.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The Easterlings attack Dale and Erebor.** Sauron's northern army advanced against the allies at the foot of the Mountain.
2. **Dáin and Brand are killed.** The two kings fell in the fighting outside Erebor's gates as their forces were driven back.
3. **The Dwarves withdraw into the Mountain.** Thorin Stonehelm and the survivors joined the Men of Dale behind Erebor's defenses.
4. **The Easterlings besiege Erebor.** The defenders held out while the larger war continued in the south and at the Black Gate.
5. **Sauron's defeat ends the siege.** After the Ring was destroyed, the besiegers lost their purpose. The defenders sallied forth and defeated them; Thorin Stonehelm became King under the Mountain.

---

## Outcome and Significance

The siege ended in a victory for the defenders, but it followed the deaths of both allied kings and the loss of Dale. The Dwarves and Men of Dale survived within Erebor until Sauron's fall broke the enemy's campaign. Their resistance prevented the Easterlings from taking the Mountain and preserved the northern kingdoms.

The northern fighting complements the War of the Ring's main narrative in Gondor. It also demonstrates that Sauron's strategy involved simultaneous attacks in distant regions. Read alongside the Battle of Dale, it presents one connected campaign with distinct phases: the loss of the city, the siege, and the counterattack after Sauron's defeat.

## Editorial Note

“Battle of Erebor” is used for the northern fighting and defense of the Mountain, while the siege is treated as the aftermath of the initial battle. The attack on Dale is covered separately to distinguish Brand's defense from the Dwarves' holdout.
