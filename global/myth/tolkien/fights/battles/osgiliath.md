# Battle of Osgiliath

*Sauron's opening assault on Gondor in the War of the Ring · 3018 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of Osgiliath was Sauron's first open assault on Gondor during the War of the Ring. His forces attacked the ruined city and its crossing over the Anduin. Gondor's defenders lost the eastern side of the river, but broke the bridge to prevent Sauron's army from using it to advance directly on Minas Tirith.

## Causes and Context

Osgiliath had once been Gondor's capital, but centuries of war and decline had left it partly ruined and divided by the Anduin. The city's bridges remained strategically important: a force crossing there could threaten the road to Minas Tirith and the defenses of western Gondor.

Sauron's attack tested Gondor's river defenses and began the open war in the south. The assault did not capture Minas Tirith or end Gondor's resistance. It established a foothold east of the river and forced the defenders to prepare for a larger invasion.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Sauron's forces attack the crossing.** Under cover of darkness and surprise, enemy troops crossed the Anduin and struck the Gondorian defenders at Osgiliath.
2. **The defenders contest the city.** Boromir and Faramir were among the captains responsible for the defense. The fighting centered on the river crossing and the remaining positions in the ruined city.
3. **Gondor withdraws from the east bank.** The defenders were forced to abandon the eastern side of Osgiliath as the enemy gained ground.
4. **The bridge is destroyed.** Gondor broke the crossing to prevent Sauron's forces from moving over the river in strength. The enemy retained the eastern bank, but its immediate advance toward Minas Tirith was checked.
5. **The war escalates.** Gondor continued to hold the western defenses until Sauron's larger assault in 3019 led to the siege of Minas Tirith and the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](./pelennor-fields.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The battle left Osgiliath divided and exposed Gondor to further attacks, but the destruction of the bridge denied Sauron an intact crossing. The city's loss was strategic rather than the fall of a functioning capital: it had been a ruin for centuries. Its position on the Anduin nevertheless made it a crucial part of Gondor's defense.

The attack also brought Boromir's report to the Council of Elrond into context. Gondor's leaders knew that Sauron's strength was growing, even as the Ring's location and the purpose of the assault remained unclear to them. The 3018 battle is separate from the later crossing battles during the siege of Minas Tirith.

## Editorial Note

Osgiliath was already partly ruined when the battle was fought. This article distinguishes the 3018 assault from the later fighting around the city during the 3019 siege of Minas Tirith.
