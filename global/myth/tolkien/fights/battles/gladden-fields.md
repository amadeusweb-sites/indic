# Battle of the Gladden Fields

*Isildur's ambush and the loss of the One Ring · 2 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

At the Gladden Fields, Orcs ambushed Isildur and his company as they travelled north from Gondor. Isildur was carrying the One Ring, which he had taken from Sauron at the end of the Second Age. The attack destroyed his guard, killed Isildur, and caused the Ring to be lost in the Anduin.

## Causes and Context

After Sauron's defeat in the War of the Last Alliance, Isildur took the One Ring and became High King of the Dúnedain in Middle-earth. He set out from Gondor to take up his northern rule, accompanied by his sons and a company of men. The route north followed the Anduin through a region where the survivors had little protection from Orcs.

The ambush was a military disaster and a turning point in the history of the Ring. Isildur's death left the northern kingdom without its king, while the Ring disappeared from the knowledge of the Dúnedain. The fuller account in *Unfinished Tales* records more detail than the brief entries in the appendices.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Isildur travels north.** He left Gondor after the Last Alliance, carrying the One Ring and accompanied by his sons and a guard.
2. **Orcs attack at the Gladden Fields.** The company was ambushed beside the Anduin. The attackers overwhelmed the Dúnedain and cut down most of the guard.
3. **Ohtar is sent away.** Isildur ordered Ohtar to escape with the shards of Narsil, preserving the sword for Isildur's heirs.
4. **Isildur attempts to flee.** He entered the river and used the Ring to escape, but the Ring slipped from his finger. Orc archers saw him and killed him.
5. **The survivors carry the news north.** Ohtar and a small number of survivors reached the Dúnedain. The Ring remained lost in the river until Déagol found it many centuries later.

## Outcome and Significance

The disaster ended Isildur's life and removed the High King from the North. Ohtar's escape preserved the shards of Narsil, which remained an heirloom of Isildur's line and were later reforged as Andúril. The Ring's loss prevented Sauron from recovering it for many centuries, though it did not destroy the power he had placed in it.

The Ring's later discovery by Déagol and Sméagol began the chain of events that eventually brought it to Bilbo and Frodo. The Gladden Fields therefore connect the end of the Second Age to the [War of the Ring](../wars/ring.md), while also marking the political fragility of Isildur's new northern realm.

## Editorial Note

The Ring was lost, not destroyed, at the Gladden Fields. Ohtar's escape with the shards of Narsil is a separate survival from the Ring's fate and should not be conflated with it.
