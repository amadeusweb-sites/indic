# Battle of Azanulbizar

*The climactic battle of the War of the Dwarves and Orcs · 2799 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of Azanulbizar was fought in the valley of the Dimrill Dale before the East-gate of Moria. Dwarves from several houses gathered there to avenge the murder of Thrór by Azog and to end the Orc threat in the Misty Mountains. The Dwarves won, but their losses were so severe that they abandoned the attempt to retake Moria.

## Causes and Context

In 2790, Thrór returned to Moria and was killed by the Orc Azog, who then mutilated his body. The murder and insult provoked a Dwarven campaign against the Orcs throughout the Misty Mountains. The fighting lasted nine years and culminated at Azanulbizar, where the assembled Dwarven forces confronted Azog's host.

The battle was both a war of vengeance and an attempt to regain the Dwarves' ancestral home. These aims did not have the same outcome: Azog and his immediate power were destroyed, but the survivors judged that the cost of entering Moria would be too high.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The Dwarven armies reach Azanulbizar.** Warriors from several Dwarven houses assembled in the valley before the East-gate. The Orcs held the slopes and the approach to Moria.
2. **The Dwarves attack uphill.** The Orcs initially held the advantage of the ground, and the Dwarven assault suffered heavily. Thorin's shield was broken; he used an oak branch in its place, earning the name Oakenshield.
3. **The battle turns.** Dáin Ironfoot led a successful attack against the Orcs. The fighting moved toward the East-gate, where Azog attempted to escape.
4. **Dáin kills Azog.** Dáin pursued Azog to the gate and killed him, avenging Thrór. The death ended Azog's leadership but not the danger inside Moria.
5. **The Dwarves refuse to enter Moria.** Looking into the darkness beyond the gate, Dáin warned Thorin that a greater terror remained within. The victors withdrew rather than attempt to reclaim the halls.

## Outcome and Significance

Azog's death fulfilled the immediate purpose of the campaign, but the Dwarves did not retake Moria. The battle's losses left the surviving army unwilling to press into the mines, and Dáin's warning confirmed that the Orcs were not the only danger there. The ancient halls remained under Orc control until Balin's later expedition, which also ended in disaster.

The battle shaped Thorin's later history and the Dwarves' continuing exile. It also explains why the Dwarves celebrated a victory without treating it as the recovery of their kingdom. The conflict that began with Thrór's murder and ended at Azanulbizar is covered more broadly in the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](../wars/dwarves-and-orcs.md).

## Editorial Note

The battle ended the feud with Azog but did not recover Moria. The Dwarves' decision to withdraw and Dáin's warning are essential to distinguishing victory at Azanulbizar from the later, unsuccessful attempt to re-establish a colony in the mines.
