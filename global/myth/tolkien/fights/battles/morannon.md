# Battle of the Morannon

*The final diversion before the Black Gate · 25 March 3019*

---

## Overview

The Battle of the Morannon was fought before the Black Gate as Aragorn led the West's remaining forces into Mordor. The march was a deliberate diversion: it drew Sauron's attention away from Frodo and Sam, who were carrying the One Ring toward Mount Doom. The army was outnumbered and could not win by conventional conquest.

## Causes and Context

After the victory on the Pelennor, the captains of the West knew they could not defeat Sauron in a direct campaign. Their hope depended on the Ring-bearer reaching Mount Doom unseen. Aragorn's decision to march to the Black Gate was intended to make Sauron believe that the Ring was being carried openly by his enemies.

The march placed the army before Mordor's main entrance in full view of the enemy. Its purpose was to occupy Sauron's attention and give Frodo and Sam time; it was not an attempt to besiege the Black Gate or conquer Mordor.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The West marches to the Black Gate.** Aragorn led the remaining forces of Gondor and Rohan into Mordor's sight.
2. **The Mouth of Sauron speaks.** Sauron's lieutenant came out of the gate and offered terms, showing Frodo's mithril coat as evidence that the enemy had captured him.
3. **The captains reject the terms.** Gandalf refused to surrender. The army understood that Frodo might be captured or dead, but continued the diversion.
4. **Sauron's host attacks.** The army of the West was surrounded and pressed hard by the forces of Mordor.
5. **The Ring is destroyed.** At Mount Doom, the Ring fell into the fire. Sauron's power collapsed, the Nazgûl perished, and the hosts of Mordor lost their command and broke.

| Mordor | Sauron's forces and the Mouth of Sauron | Attacked the army before the gate and sought to compel its surrender |

---

## Outcome and Significance

The battle's strategic aim succeeded even though the army of the West faced imminent defeat. The Ring was destroyed while Sauron's forces were focused on Aragorn's host. With the Ring gone, Sauron's power failed, Barad-dûr fell, and the army before the Black Gate was scattered.

The outcome did not come from the West's military strength; it depended on the Ring-bearer's concealed mission. The Morannon was therefore the final battle of the War of the Ring, but its decisive cause was the destruction of the Ring at Mount Doom.

## Editorial Note

The Battle of the Morannon and the destruction of the Ring are separate events in different places. The latter caused the sudden collapse that ended the former.
