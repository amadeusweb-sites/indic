# Second Battle of the Fords of Isen

*The collapse of Rohan's western defense · 2 March 3019*

---

## Overview

The Second Battle of the Fords of Isen was Saruman's renewed assault on Rohan's western frontier. With Théoden and the main force away, the Rohirrim at the crossings fought under divided command. Saruman's army forced the fords, scattered the defenders, and left Erkenbrand to gather survivors toward Helm's Deep.

## Causes and Context

After Théodred's death in the first battle, Rohan's western forces were weakened and the command structure remained difficult. Théoden had taken the main army east, while Saruman prepared another assault from Isengard. The river crossings remained the key point: if Saruman's forces crossed in strength, they could advance into the Westfold.

Erkenbrand was not commanding at the fords during the battle. Grimbold and Elfhelm led the forces there, while Erkenbrand gathered men elsewhere. This distinction explains why the Rohirrim fought as separated groups and why their withdrawal toward Helm's Deep did not immediately become a unified retreat.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **Saruman renews the attack.** His army advanced against the crossings after the first battle had weakened Rohan's defenses.
2. **The Rohirrim defend in separate forces.** Grimbold and Elfhelm held different positions around the fords. The divided command made it harder to respond as a single army.
3. **The crossings are forced.** Saruman's forces broke the Rohirrim's line and crossed the Isen. The defenders were scattered and suffered heavy losses.
4. **Grimbold leads a rearguard.** He protected the withdrawal of part of the force rather than attempting to hold the river after the position had been lost.
5. **The survivors regroup west of Helm's Deep.** Erkenbrand gathered scattered companies in the Westfold. Gandalf later found the survivors and led them toward the Hornburg, where they joined the defense and relief of the [Battle of the Hornburg](./hornburg.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The defeat opened Rohan's western approaches and left the Rohirrim unable to stop Saruman's army at the river. The surviving captains' rearguard and regrouping nevertheless preserved men who could later help defend the Hornburg. Gandalf's arrival with Erkenbrand's gathered force became a crucial part of the relief at Helm's Deep.

The battle should be read alongside the first engagement on 25 February, not as a continuation of fighting on the same day. Together, the two defeats explain the loss of Théodred, the disorganization of Rohan's western command, and the conditions under which Théoden faced Saruman's army at Helm's Deep.

## Editorial Note

Erkenbrand gathered men away from the crossings and was not present as the field commander in the battle. His later arrival with Gandalf belongs to the relief of Helm's Deep.
