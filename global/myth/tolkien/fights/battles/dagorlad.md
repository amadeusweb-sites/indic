# Battle of Dagorlad

*The Last Alliance enters Mordor · 3434 of the Second Age*

---

## Overview

The Battle of Dagorlad was the Last Alliance's decisive victory over Sauron's field army outside Mordor. Gil-galad and Elendil led the allied Elves and Men against Sauron's forces on the plain before the Black Gate. The victory opened the way into Mordor, but it began a seven-year siege rather than ending the war.

## Causes and Context

Sauron had rebuilt his power in Mordor after the fall of Númenor and renewed his war against the free peoples of Middle-earth. Gil-galad and Elendil formed the Last Alliance, combining Elvish and human forces for a direct campaign against him. Their aim was not merely to defend a border but to overthrow Sauron's power at its source.

The Alliance advanced from the west and met Sauron's army on Dagorlad, the plain outside Mordor's Black Gate. The battle's importance came from what it made possible: the victors could pass into Mordor and bring the fight to Barad-dûr.

---

## Course of the Battle

1. **The Alliance advances to Mordor.** Gil-galad and Elendil led the allied host eastward to the plain before the Black Gate.
2. **The armies meet on Dagorlad.** Sauron's forces opposed the Alliance outside Mordor. The surviving summaries identify the scale and result of the battle but give few tactical details.
3. **Sauron's host is defeated.** The Last Alliance won the field and forced a path through the Black Gate.
4. **The siege begins.** The victors entered Mordor and laid siege to Barad-dûr. Sauron remained inside the fortress, and the war continued for seven years.
5. **The final struggle follows.** The siege ended only when Sauron came forth to fight. Gil-galad and Elendil were killed; Isildur cut the One Ring from Sauron's hand after they had fallen.

## Outcome and Significance

Dagorlad was the decisive field victory of the Last Alliance, not the final defeat of Sauron. The battle enabled the Alliance to enter Mordor, but Barad-dûr resisted a long siege. The distinction matters: the battle opened the campaign's final phase, while Sauron's defeat came only after the siege and the deaths of the Alliance's leaders.

Isildur's taking of the Ring followed the deaths of Gil-galad and Elendil. His decision not to destroy it meant that Sauron's power was diminished but not ended permanently. The battle and siege therefore form the immediate historical background to the Ring's later loss in the [Gladden Fields](./gladden-fields.md).

## Editorial Note

The published chronology gives Dagorlad's date and outcome, while the appendices summarize the campaign that followed. This article distinguishes the field battle from the later siege and does not invent a detailed tactical narrative unsupported by those accounts.
