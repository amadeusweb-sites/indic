# Sack of Gondolin

*Morgoth's plundering of the Hidden City · 510 of the First Age*

---

## Overview

The Sack of Gondolin was the destruction and plundering of Turgon's hidden city after Morgoth discovered its location through Maeglin's betrayal. Morgoth's forces overwhelmed the city, killed many of its people, and ended Gondolin's role as a refuge and military power. Tuor and Idril led a remnant through a secret way into the mountains.

## Causes and Context

Gondolin had remained hidden in the Encircling Mountains for centuries. Its secrecy protected it from Morgoth, but Maeglin was captured beyond the city's borders and revealed its location and defenses. Morgoth prepared a surprise attack with Orcs, Balrogs, and dragons.

The sack is the taking and destruction of the city; the [Fall of Gondolin](../battles/gondolin.md) page focuses on the fighting and the escape. Both refer to the events of 510, but emphasize different aspects of the catastrophe.

---

## Course of the Sack

1. **Morgoth discovers the city's location.** Maeglin's betrayal gave the enemy knowledge of Gondolin.
2. **The city is assaulted.** Morgoth's host broke into Gondolin during a festival and fought through its defenses.
3. **The city is plundered and destroyed.** Many inhabitants were killed, and the hidden realm ceased to exist.
4. **Survivors escape.** Tuor and Idril led a company through a concealed route to the south. Their son Eärendil was among those who escaped.
5. **The survivors carry Gondolin's history forward.** Eärendil's later voyage would lead to the appeal that brought the Valar into the War of Wrath.

## Outcome and Significance

Gondolin's destruction removed one of the last great Noldorin kingdoms from Beleriand. The loss of the city and its people was severe, but the survivors preserved a lineage that connected the Houses of Hador and Finwë. Eärendil's survival became important to the later appeal for aid from the Valar.

The sack demonstrates the limits of Gondolin's secrecy once its location was betrayed. The survivors did not establish a replacement city, and the fall did not itself defeat Morgoth; it preceded the final War of Wrath.
