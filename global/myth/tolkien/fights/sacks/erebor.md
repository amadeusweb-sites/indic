# Sack of Erebor

*Smaug seizes the Dwarves' kingdom · 2770 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Sack of Erebor was Smaug's seizure of the Lonely Mountain and its treasure. The dragon drove Thrór, Thráin, and the Dwarves of Durin's Folk from their halls, ending the prosperous kingdom beneath the Mountain. Smaug's attack also destroyed the neighboring city of Dale.

## Causes and Context

The Dwarves of Durin's Folk had rebuilt Erebor as a wealthy kingdom, developing its mines and trade with Dale. Smaug learned of its riches and came from the north to claim them. The assault destroyed the Dwarven settlement and the neighboring human city in a single catastrophe.

The exiles retained their identity and craft, but for generations they had no settled home. Thrór, Thráin, and later Thorin carried the memory of Erebor and the desire to reclaim it.

---

## Course of the Sack

1. **Smaug attacks the Mountain.** The dragon came upon Erebor and used his fire to overwhelm the Dwarves.
2. **The kingdom is plundered.** Smaug seized the halls and treasure and made the Mountain his lair.
3. **The Dwarves flee.** Thrór, Thráin, and their people escaped, becoming a wandering community.
4. **The region is left under the dragon's control.** Smaug occupied Erebor and Dale remained in ruins.
5. **The Dwarves return.** Bilbo's expedition led to Smaug's death and the restoration of Erebor, followed by the Battle of the Five Armies.

## Outcome and Significance

Smaug's occupation removed the Dwarves from Erebor and destroyed the political and economic partnership between the Mountain and Dale. The exiles' long displacement shaped Thorin's quest to reclaim his inheritance. The return succeeded, but Smaug's death exposed the treasure to a new conflict among Dwarves, Men, and Elves.

The Sack of Erebor and the [Sack of Dale](./dale.md) describe two consequences of one attack. Erebor's recovery came nearly two centuries later and should not be confused with the sack itself.
