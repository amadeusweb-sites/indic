# Sack of Dale

*Smaug's attack on the city of Men · 2770 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The Sack of Dale was Smaug's destruction of the city beneath the Lonely Mountain. The dragon descended from Erebor, burned the homes of the Men of Dale, and killed King Girion. The survivors fled south or took refuge elsewhere, while Smaug occupied the Mountain and ended the Dwarves' kingdom there.

## Causes and Context

Dale had grown beside Erebor through trade with the Dwarves. Smaug was drawn to the wealth of the Mountain and attacked the region, striking both settlements. His assault on Dale was part of the same catastrophe as the [Sack of Erebor](./erebor.md), but the two entries describe different peoples and losses.

The fall of Dale deprived the North of a major human city and drove its inhabitants from their homes. Members of Girion's family survived, including Bard, who later returned to Dale after Smaug's death.

---

## Course of the Sack

1. **Smaug descends on the region.** The dragon attacked the settlements below the Lonely Mountain.
2. **Dale is burned.** Smaug destroyed the city and killed King Girion.
3. **The people flee.** Survivors escaped south to Lake-town or scattered elsewhere, carrying the memory and claim of Girion's house.
4. **The city remains abandoned.** Smaug's occupation of Erebor prevented Dale from being rebuilt for nearly two centuries.
5. **Bard restores Dale.** After killing Smaug during the events of *The Hobbit*, Bard led the rebuilding of the city and became its king.

## Outcome and Significance

Dale and Erebor were both destroyed as centers of power, leaving the region dominated by Smaug. The survivors of Dale became refugees, and Girion's descendants preserved the claim to the city until Bard returned after the dragon's death.

The sack also set the conditions for the later Battle of the Five Armies. Smaug's death removed the threat but exposed the treasure of Erebor and created a new dispute among Dwarves, Men, and Elves. Dale's rebuilding and Erebor's restoration were related consequences, but they were not part of the sack itself.
