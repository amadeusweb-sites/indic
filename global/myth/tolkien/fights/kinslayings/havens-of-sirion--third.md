# Third Kinslaying at the Havens of Sirion

*The final assault by the sons of Fëanor · 538 of the First Age*

---

## Overview

The Third Kinslaying was the assault on the Havens of Sirion by the surviving sons of Fëanor. Elwing and the refugees living at the Havens guarded the Silmaril that had escaped the [Second Kinslaying at Doriath](./doriath--second.md). When the Fëanorians renewed their demand for the jewel, Elwing and her people refused to surrender it.

The attack came in 538 of the First Age, as the long war against Morgoth approached its end. The sons of Fëanor gained neither the Silmaril nor a lasting peace: Elwing cast herself into the sea and was rescued by Ulmo, while Elrond and Elros were taken and later fostered by Maglor. The surviving people of the Havens were scattered, but the assault did not erase every remnant of the Elves and Men of Beleriand.

## Causes and Context

The Havens of Sirion had become a refuge for those displaced by the destruction of Beleriand. Elwing, daughter of Dior, brought there the Silmaril that she had saved from the Second Kinslaying. She later married Eärendil, and their sons Elrond and Elros were born at the Havens.

The sons of Fëanor persisted in the oath they had sworn before the earlier Kinslayings. Four of them remained: Maedhros, Maglor, Amrod, and Amras. They sent word to Elwing demanding the Silmaril. Elwing and her people refused, and the sons of Fëanor chose to attack rather than abandon their claim.

---

## Course of the Kinslaying

1. **The demand is renewed.** The sons of Fëanor sent a message to Elwing at the Havens, asserting their claim to the Silmaril under their oath. Elwing would not surrender the jewel, which had been entrusted to her through the ruin of her family and realm.

2. **The sons of Fëanor attack.** Maedhros, Maglor, Amrod, and Amras led their followers against the Havens. The fighting broke apart the refuge where Elves and Men from several fallen realms had gathered.

3. **Elwing escapes into the sea.** Rather than allow the attackers to take the Silmaril, Elwing cast herself into the sea with the jewel. Ulmo rescued her, and in the form of a great white bird she found Eärendil aboard Vingilot. The Silmaril was not captured in the assault.

4. **Elrond and Elros are taken.** The attackers captured Elwing and Eärendil's young sons. Maglor took the children into his care and treated them with love, despite the violence that had made them captives.

5. **The refugees scatter.** The Havens were left in ruin, and the survivors fled. The assault ended Sirion's role as a refuge for the displaced peoples of Beleriand, but survivors remained elsewhere and the wider struggle against Morgoth continued.

6. **The attack leads to Eärendil's voyage.** Reunited with Elwing and carrying the Silmaril, Eärendil sailed to Aman to seek aid for Elves and Men. His appeal brought the Host of the West into the War of Wrath.

## Outcome and Significance

The Third Kinslaying was the final attack by the sons of Fëanor to recover the Silmaril, but they did not obtain it. Elwing survived and reunited with Eärendil; their voyage to Aman brought the appeal that led to Morgoth's defeat in the War of Wrath. The violence at Sirion therefore stands at the end of the Silmaril's long passage through the histories of Beren and Lúthien, Dior, and Elwing.

The capture of Elrond and Elros also shaped the history of the Second and Third Ages. Maglor's fostering of the twins is presented as an act of care within a story otherwise marked by the Fëanorians' oath and violence. The Havens' destruction is also treated as a [Sack of the Havens of Sirion](../sacks/havens-of-sirion.md), a companion page focused on the ruin of the settlement.

## Editorial Note

This account distinguishes the destruction of the Havens from the extinction of all resistance in Beleriand: refugees escaped, and the War of Wrath followed. Elwing's rescue by Ulmo and the twins' later upbringing by Maglor are kept distinct from the attackers' failure to seize the Silmaril.
