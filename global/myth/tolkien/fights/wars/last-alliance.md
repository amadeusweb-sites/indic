# War of the Last Alliance

*The final alliance against Sauron · 3429–3441 of the Second Age*

---

## Overview

The War of the Last Alliance was the final great campaign against Sauron in the Second Age. Gil-galad, High King of the Noldor, and Elendil, leader of the Dúnedain in Middle-earth, joined their peoples in a military alliance. They entered Mordor after the victory at Dagorlad and besieged Barad-dûr for seven years. Sauron was overthrown, but Isildur kept the One Ring, allowing Sauron's power to endure.

## Causes and Context

After the Downfall of Númenor, Elendil and his sons founded realms in Middle-earth, while Sauron returned to Mordor and rebuilt his strength. In 3429 he attacked Gondor and captured Minas Ithil, forcing Isildur to flee north. Anárion continued the defense from the western side of the Anduin.

Gil-galad and Elendil had already recognized the danger Sauron posed. The assault on Minas Ithil made war unavoidable, and the two leaders joined their forces for a campaign into Mordor rather than attempt to defend each kingdom separately.

---

## Course of the War

1. **Sauron attacks Gondor.** The capture of Minas Ithil opened the war and threatened the new Númenórean realms. Isildur escaped with his family and the White Tree; Anárion held the west.

2. **The Alliance gathers.** Elendil and Gil-galad assembled the Elves and Men of the West and marched east. Their army was the greatest force yet gathered in Middle-earth.

3. **Dagorlad opens Mordor.** The Alliance defeated Sauron's field army on the plain before the Black Gate. The victory allowed the allies to enter Mordor and begin the [Siege of Barad-dûr](../sieges/barad-dur.md).

4. **Barad-dûr is besieged.** The siege lasted seven years and caused heavy losses. Anárion was killed before the final confrontation, while Sauron remained within the fortress.

5. **Sauron is overthrown.** Sauron came forth and fought Gil-galad and Elendil; both died. Isildur cut the One Ring from Sauron's hand and took it for himself. Barad-dûr fell, but the Ring was not destroyed.

The field victory at Dagorlad is treated separately in the [Battle of Dagorlad](../battles/dagorlad.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The war ended Sauron's Second-Age dominion and destroyed Barad-dûr, but it did not remove the One Ring. Isildur treated it as a weregild for the deaths of his father and brother and refused to destroy it. His decision left Sauron a means of returning, while the later Disaster of the Gladden Fields caused the Ring to be lost rather than safely guarded.

The Last Alliance's victory was therefore decisive but incomplete. Its two leaders died, the kingdoms they founded continued under their heirs, and the Ring's survival connected the Second Age's final war to the [War of the Ring](./ring.md).
