# War against Angmar

*The long struggle for the North-kingdom · c. 1300–1975 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The war against Angmar was a centuries-long conflict between the northern Dúnedain and the realm of Angmar, founded by the Witch-king in the north-east of Eriador. Angmar exploited the division of Arnor into Arthedain, Cardolan, and Rhudaur, defeating the successor kingdoms in turn. Its capture of Fornost in 1974 ended Arthedain, but an allied force from Gondor, Lindon, and the surviving Dúnedain defeated Angmar the following year.

The victory destroyed Angmar as a realm, not the northern threat to the Dúnedain. Arnor was never restored; its surviving people continued under the Chieftains, while the Witch-king escaped and later returned to Sauron's service.

## Causes and Context

Arnor had divided into three rival kingdoms after the death of Eärendur: Arthedain in the west, Cardolan in the south, and Rhudaur in the east. Their disputes over borders and succession weakened their ability to act together. Angmar emerged to the north-east under the Witch-king, Sauron's chief servant, and used those divisions to isolate and attack the Dúnedain.

The conflict was not one uninterrupted siege. Angmar's power rose over generations, and the fortunes of the northern kingdoms changed through successive invasions, losses, and periods of recovery. The Great Plague further weakened Cardolan in 1636, while Arthedain remained the last surviving Dúnedain kingdom in the North.

---

## Course of the War

1. **Angmar is founded.** Around 1300, the Witch-king established Angmar in the north-east. From there he threatened the divided realms of Arnor and gained influence in Rhudaur, where the Dúnedain were fewer than the Hill-men.

2. **The northern kingdoms are divided and weakened.** Angmar's pressure fell first on Rhudaur and Cardolan. The rival kingdoms could not maintain a common defense, and the struggle over Amon Sûl and its palantír further weakened their position.

3. **The attack of 1409 breaks the Dúnedain line.** Angmar invaded Cardolan and Rhudaur, and Amon Sûl fell. Cardolan was devastated; Arthedain survived with aid from Lindon, but its territory and strength were reduced. The remaining Dúnedain of Cardolan later endured the effects of the Great Plague and further attacks.

4. **Fornost falls.** In 1974, Angmar overran Arthedain and captured Fornost. King Arvedui fled north, but was lost at sea while seeking help from the Lossoth. The fall of the city ended the North-kingdom as a political realm.

5. **The allies defeat Angmar.** In 1975, Eärnur brought an army from Gondor to the Grey Havens, where it joined the Elves of Lindon and the surviving Dúnedain. At the Battle of Fornost, Angmar's army was routed. The Witch-king fled east; when Eärnur pursued, Glorfindel restrained him with a prophecy that the Witch-king would not fall by the hand of a man.

## Outcome and Significance

The allied victory ended Angmar's power in the North, but it came too late to restore Arnor. Arthedain had fallen, Cardolan had been devastated, and Rhudaur had long since ceased to be a Dúnedain realm. Aranarth, son of Arvedui, became the first Chieftain of the Dúnedain rather than king of a restored northern kingdom.

The Witch-king survived the defeat and returned to the wider service of Sauron. The prophecy spoken by Glorfindel was fulfilled centuries later when Éowyn and Merry confronted him at the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../battles/pelennor-fields.md). The fall of Fornost and the battle that followed are separate entries: see the [Siege of Fornost](../sieges/fornost.md) and the [Battle of Fornost](../battles/fornost.md).
