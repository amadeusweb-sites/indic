# War of the Dwarves and Orcs

*The Dwarves' vengeance for Thrór · 2793–2799 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

The War of the Dwarves and Orcs was a campaign of vengeance begun after Azog killed Thrór at the East-gate of Moria. Dwarves from across the mountains attacked Orc strongholds in the Misty Mountains, and the conflict culminated at Azanulbizar in 2799. The Dwarves defeated Azog's forces, but the cost was so high that they abandoned the attempt to retake Moria.

## Causes and Context

In 2790, Thrór returned to Moria, the ancient home of Durin's Folk, despite the danger of entering a place long held by Orcs. Azog seized and killed him at the East-gate, then marked the body with his own name. The insult was both personal and political: it challenged the Dwarves' claim to their ancestral halls and dishonored their king.

Thráin called the Dwarves to war. The conflict was unusual in scale, bringing together warriors from the seven kindreds rather than remaining a feud between one Dwarven house and a single Orc chieftain. Their shared purpose was to punish Azog and break the Orc power in the mountains; recovering Moria was a hope, but not the only war aim.

---

## Course of the War

1. **Thrór is killed.** Azog murdered Thrór at Moria's East-gate. Nár escaped and carried the news to Thráin, who resolved to avenge his father.

2. **The Dwarves gather.** Thráin summoned the Dwarven kindreds. After preparations, they began attacking Orcs in the Misty Mountains in 2793. The conflict spread through strongholds and passes, wearing down the Orcs before the final confrontation.

3. **The armies meet at Azanulbizar.** In 2799, the Dwarves gathered in the valley before Moria's East-gate. The fighting was severe, and the Dwarves were driven back at first. Thorin Oakenshield's shield was broken; he used an oak branch as a substitute, giving him the name by which he later became known.

4. **Azog is killed.** Náin was slain at the gate, but his son Dáin pursued Azog and killed him. The Orcs were defeated, and the Dwarves had avenged Thrór.

5. **Moria is left unreclaimed.** Dáin entered the threshold, saw the Balrog's presence, and warned Thráin against trying to retake the halls. The Dwarves buried their dead and withdrew rather than risk another battle inside Moria. The engagement is covered separately in the [Battle of Azanulbizar](../battles/azanulbizar.md).

## Outcome and Significance

The war ended with the Orcs defeated in the open and Azog dead, but it did not restore Moria to the Dwarves. The loss of so many warriors made an assault on the underground halls impossible, and Dáin's warning made clear that an even greater danger remained within. Moria stayed under Orc control until Balin's later expedition, which also ended in disaster.

The conflict shaped Thorin's life and the history of his people. The survivors remained scattered, and Thorin's later quest to reclaim Erebor was undertaken by a people still marked by exile and loss. The War of the Dwarves and Orcs was therefore a victory of vengeance, not a recovery of the Dwarves' lost kingdom.
