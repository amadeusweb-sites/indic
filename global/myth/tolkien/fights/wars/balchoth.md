# War against the Balchoth

*The invasion checked at the Field of Celebrant · 2510 of the Third Age*

---

## Overview

In 2510 of the Third Age, the Balchoth, an Easterling people from beyond the Sea of Rhûn, crossed the Anduin in force and ravaged Calenardhon. Gondor's northern army was driven back toward the Limlight. With the force in danger, Steward Cirion sent north for help from Eorl and the Éothéod. Eorl's ride south and arrival at the Field of Celebrant turned the campaign and saved Gondor's army.

The invasion's defeat also led to a lasting political settlement: Cirion granted Calenardhon to the Éothéod, who settled there as the Rohirrim. This article follows the invasion and its aftermath; the linked [Battle of the Field of Celebrant](../battles/field-of-celebrant.md) focuses on the decisive engagement itself.

## Causes and Context

The Balchoth came from beyond the Sea of Rhûn, east of Gondor. The accounts identify the invasion and its route across the Anduin, but do not explain the Balchoth's political aims or name a ruler who directed them. Their attack struck Calenardhon, a sparsely peopled province that Gondor could not readily reinforce from its southern heartland.

Cirion's appeal went to the Éothéod, a Northman people living far to the north. The distance made a quick response uncertain, but Eorl chose to ride south with his people. The accounts preserve the broad course of the campaign rather than a full military record: they do not supply a detailed order of battle, casualty figures, or the names of Balchoth commanders.

The invasion is best understood as a war with one recorded decisive battle, not as a sequence of separately named engagements. The Field of Celebrant broke the Balchoth's advance; Cirion's grant and the oath between him and Eorl belong to the political aftermath.

---

## Course of the War

1. **The invasion crosses the Anduin.** The Balchoth came over the Anduin in large numbers and ravaged Calenardhon. Gondor's northern army confronted them but was forced back toward the Limlight.

2. **Cirion calls on the Éothéod.** As the Gondorian position became critical, Cirion sent a message north to Eorl. The fuller account in *Unfinished Tales* follows Borondir, the messenger who reached the Éothéod and delivered the Steward's appeal.

3. **Eorl answers.** Eorl decided to aid Gondor and led a mounted host south. The Éothéod's rapid journey brought them to the Field of Celebrant while the Gondorian army was still under pressure.

4. **The Balchoth are routed.** Eorl's arrival and attack on the Balchoth's flank changed the course of the battle. The invaders were routed, and Gondor's northern army was saved. The surviving accounts do not give a reliable casualty total or a detailed account of the Balchoth's retreat.

5. **Cirion grants Calenardhon.** In gratitude for Eorl's aid, Cirion offered the province to the Éothéod. Eorl accepted on behalf of his people, who later moved south and settled the land.

6. **The alliance is sworn.** Cirion and Eorl met at Amon Anwar, later called Halifirien, and swore an oath of friendship and mutual aid. The settlement established the bond between Gondor and the new people of Rohan.

## Outcome and Significance

The victory ended the invasion and saved Gondor's northern army, but its importance reaches beyond the battlefield. Cirion's grant allowed the Éothéod to settle Calenardhon, which became the land of Rohan. The oath at Amon Anwar established a continuing alliance: centuries later, Rohan answered Gondor's call in the [War of the Ring](./ring.md), most notably at the [Pelennor Fields](../battles/pelennor-fields.md).

The war page and the battle page describe different scopes. This page covers the Balchoth invasion, Cirion's appeal, Eorl's response, and the resulting settlement; the battle page concentrates on the fighting at the Field of Celebrant. The surviving narrative is brief, so no additional battles, commanders, motives, or casualty totals should be inferred.
